M1 · Special Functions for Scattering

Part 0 — Foundations · Status: draft v1 · Companion notebook: M1_special_functions.ipynb

Learning goals. After this chapter you should be able to

  • separate the Schrödinger equation in spherical coordinates and explain why angular momentum is
    quantized;
  • use Legendre polynomials and spherical harmonics: recurrences, orthogonality, parity, the
    addition theorem and the large-lll asymptotic form;
  • work with the spherical Bessel, Neumann and Hankel functions and their Riccati forms, and know
    when their asymptotic forms may be used;
  • expand a plane wave in partial waves and estimate how many terms are needed;
  • handle the Gamma function of complex argument and compute the Coulomb phase shift safely;
  • recognise Coulomb wave functions as confluent hypergeometric functions, with their logarithmic
    phase, turning point and penetration factor;
  • know where Airy and cylindrical Bessel functions appear in scattering;
  • evaluate all of these stably: recurrence direction, Miller’s algorithm, continued fractions and
    Steed’s method.

Prerequisites: calculus with series and ordinary differential equations, complex numbers, and
separation of variables for the hydrogen atom.

Sources: C&H §2.1 and §3.2–3.4; the NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions (DLMF),
chapters 5, 9, 10, 13, 14 and 33; Barnett (1981) for Steed’s method.

How to read this chapter. Every scattering calculation in these notes is built from the
functions below. You do not need to memorize the formulas, but you should know what each function
looks like, which limits it obeys, and how a computer evaluates it reliably. The notebook checks
every identity quoted here numerically.

Why λ=l(l+1)\lambda = l(l+1)λ=l(l+1). Take m=0m = 0m=0 and try a power series Θ=∑kakxk\Theta = \sum_k a_kx^kΘ=∑k​ak​xk. Substituting

M1.8 Evaluating special functions numerically

Two libraries cover almost everything in these notes. scipy.special is fast, works in double
precision and mostly takes real parameters. mpmath works in arbitrary precision and takes complex
parameters, but is slow, so it serves as the reference. The real art is in two techniques that both
libraries use internally: choosing the direction of recurrences, and continued fractions.

M1.8.1 The direction of a recurrence

The three-term recurrence (M1.16), fl+1=2l+1ρfl−fl−1f_{l+1} = \frac{2l+1}{\rho}f_l - f_{l-1}fl+1​=ρ2l+1​fl​−fl−1​, is satisfied by jlj_ljl​
and by nln_lnl​. For l>ρl > \rhol>ρ, jlj_ljl​ decreases with lll and nln_lnl​ grows: jlj_ljl​ is the minimal
solution and nln_lnl​ the dominant one. If jlj_ljl​ is computed upwards from j0j_0j0​ and j1j_1j1​, each rounding
error mixes in a little nln_lnl​. Its relative size then grows like nl/jl∼[(2l−1)!!]2/ρ2l+1n_l/j_l \sim [(2l-1)!!]^2/\rho^{2l+1}nl​/jl​∼[(2l−1)!!]2/ρ2l+1.
Figure M1.8 shows the result. At ρ=1\rho = 1ρ=1 the upward recurrence is wrong by a factor 4×1024\times10^24×102 at
l=10l = 10l=10 and by 103210^{32}1032 at l=20l = 20l=20; at ρ=10\rho = 10ρ=10 the trouble starts once lll exceeds 10.

Miller’s algorithm turns this around. Start at some NNN well beyond both lll and ρ\rhoρ with
arbitrary values (say fN+1=0f_{N+1} = 0fN+1​=0, fN=10−300f_N = 10^{-300}fN​=10−300) and run the recurrence downwards. In that
direction jlj_ljl​ is dominant, so any admixture of nln_lnl​ dies out. Finally rescale everything with the
known value j0=sin⁡ρ/ρj_0 = \sin\rho/\rhoj0​=sinρ/ρ. The errors stay at 10−1510^{-15}10−15 for every lll. The general rule (see
Gautschi 1967):

Run a recurrence in the direction in which the wanted solution grows.

For Coulomb functions, FlF_lFl​ decreases with lll inside the turning points and GlG_lGl​ increases. So
FlF_lFl​ is computed downwards and GlG_lGl​ upwards.

M1.8.3 How to trust a special-function routine

Check it against independent information:

  • identities that must hold exactly: Wronskians, recurrences, the addition theorem;
  • known limits: small and large arguments, and the reduction of FlF_lFl​ to j^l\hat j_lj^​l​ at η=0\eta = 0η=0;
  • an independent high-precision reference such as mpmath or DLMF tables.

The test suite of scatlib does all three for every function used in these notes.
